## 1. The three most defensible causes

**A. Market and crypto-sector timing: about 40–65% of the loss. Medium confidence that it is the largest piece; low confidence that it is a flaw that would repeat.**
- The market part depends on the benchmark:
  - Equal-weight benchmark: -$228. Of that, a standing net-long tilt explains only -$27; the other -$202 is timing.
  - Beta-weighted benchmark: -$349 to -$366 (-3.6%, t -1.81).
  - Crypto group alone: crypto net exposure × crypto basket return = -$270 (-2.8%, t -1.89). This happened although average crypto net was only -$14 (-0.2%). The book was on the wrong side of the swings, not tilted.
- Five bad days (6/25, 7/22, 7/16, 6/24, 6/30) cost -$399. The five best days made +$345.
- Net exposure barely predicts the next 24h: rho -0.08 to -0.10, standard error about 0.14. The circular-shift test gives p 0.07.
- The earlier claim that the book was "structurally net long" is refuted as a cause. The net long sat in megacaps, and its standing part earned +0.70%.
- Crypto names have betas of 1.6–1.9 against 0.2–0.7 for megacaps. That is why an equal-weight split understates the market part.

**B. A flat 5% stop and sizing that ignores volatility sent most of the loss through four crypto names. Medium confidence on the concentration, low on it being the cause.**
- Stop rate on MSTR, COIN, HOOD and CRCL was 12 of 22 trips, against 9 of 43 elsewhere (Fisher p 0.011). The baseline shows the same: 23/78 against 13/132 (p 0.001).
- Those four names account for -$361, or 63% of the trip loss.
- The 19–21 stop-outs total -$704 to -$767, but that split is true by construction: a trip is only labelled "stopped" after it has lost 5%.
- Holding instead of stopping would have changed P&L by -$3 at 24h (t -0.02). The stops themselves are neutral.
- Shorts lost -$386 against -$154 for longs, despite being only 31% of position-hours. After beta the two legs look alike: shorts -1.07% (t -1.04), longs -0.83%. The baseline has the same short-side weakness (-1.79% after beta, t -3.05), so it comes from negative cards on names that have already fallen, not from how the LLM reasons.

**C. Stock selection, driven mainly by META: -$189 to -$311 depending on the benchmark (t -1.0 to -1.4). Low confidence.**

## 3. Candidate changes, ranked

Hedge turnover is about 8–9× equity, roughly 0.5% extra fees. On the baseline, group hedging makes Sharpe worse: t goes from -1.77 to -2.36.

**Change 1: a deterministic neutralisation and volatility-scaling step in the risk layer, applied after the LLM.** This is the one to do.
- **Exact change:**
  1. Remove the beta-weighted net exposure using betas estimated before the window, or make the crypto group and the megacap group each net-neutral. Index perps are not a usable hedge (R² 0.01).
  2. Set each name's cap to 0.10 × (median volatility / that name's volatility).
  3. Set the stop at k × the name's daily volatility in place of the flat 5%. Fix k in advance, for example k = 2.
  4. Log each adjustment as a risk_action.
- **Mechanism:** a sentiment signal is meant to be about individual names. Market and sector timing is exposure nobody intended, and in this window it was the largest single piece of the loss. Scaling by volatility spreads risk evenly, so a few crypto names can no longer carry most of the loss.
- **Expected effect on dev:** from the table, the LLM's loss falls from -5.5% to about -2% and max drawdown roughly halves. Take off about 0.5% for hedge fees.
- **Caveats:**
  - The gain is mostly removing a timing component that was bad luck in this window (t -0.8 to -1.8). It does not create an edge.
  - Hedging a book whose stock picks lose money cuts its volatility, so Sharpe can get worse, as the baseline row shows.
  - The flat 5% stop sits only 1.1–1.3 daily standard deviations away on the crypto names. Crypto correlation of 0.82 in the test window gives a reason for the change that does not depend on dev P&L.
- **Overfitting risk:** low to medium. There are no parameters to fit if the betas, groups and k are fixed in advance, but the decision to adopt it was prompted by dev. Log it as one trial in `trials.log`.
- **Cheap test:** add a policy that replays the logged LLM `targets` through the modified `risk.apply` and `replay.run`. This makes no Qwen calls and writes only to the scratchpad. Pass criteria, declared before running: beta to the equal-weight basket near 0, fees within +0.6%, max drawdown lower. P&L is not a pass criterion. The replay ignores feedback from the book into the prompt, and a full-dev Qwen rerun is not needed for this change.

**Change 2: show the model its risk state and the price context.** This is for explainability; the expected P&L effect is about zero.
- **Exact change:** the prompt shows:
  - the per-name cap in force now (the off-hours cap cut weights on 93 of 300 decisions, although the model's reasons said it had halved)
  - which names are in cooldown and until when (41 re-requests were blocked)
  - each position's entry price and distance to its stop
  - the book's current net and beta-weighted net
  - each name's return over the last 24h and since the card became available.
- **Expected effect on dev:** every risk rule individually moved P&L by less than $20, with intervals spanning zero, so the gain is agreement between the stated reasons and the executed book. That counts toward the explainability half of the Track 2 score.
- **Overfitting risk:** low, because nothing is tuned on P&L.
- **Test:** a Qwen rerun on the first half of dev (6/22–7/15, about 144 decisions).
  - Pass if cooldown re-requests fall to near 0 and off-hours and net-cap clips fall sharply.
  - Treat P&L as noise at that sample size: the full 50-day confidence interval for the LLM-minus-baseline gap is already [-6.8%, +2.1%].
  - Before any rerun, pin the card set. `cards.parquet` in the working tree has 75 dev news cards the replay never saw, so filter to the logged evidence_ids or snapshot the file. Otherwise the comparison is confounded and the cached responses will not be reused.

**Change 3 (optional; the evidence is weak): treat analyst ratings as lagged evidence.**
- **Exact change:** label rating cards "lagged, reaction already priced" (they become available at rating_date + 1 day, 13:30 UTC), and do not let a rating card alone open a position.
- **Evidence for:** the rating component of the baseline lost -2.42% on paper (t -2.27). The LLM followed the rating's sign 77% of the time. Short spells entered on rating cards made -3.20% raw.
- **Evidence against:**
  - 60% of the baseline's rating loss falls on 3 days.
  - After beta, the rating short spells are only -0.80%.
  - Counted once per event, the median effect is about 0.
- **Overfitting risk:** high. Do not flip ratings to contrarian.
- **Test:** first an offline IC check of the baseline score with ratings removed (IC goes from -0.059 to -0.024, t -0.58), then include it in the same half-dev rerun as a separately logged trial.

**Do not do:** a blocking rule for shorts after drops, an intrabar stop, a regex for mislabelled "new" cards, momentum or reversal overlays (every momentum rule lost 4.6–13.7%), extra decision slots, or prompt tuning on the individual dev examples.

### decisions

### risk

The risk layer and execution are not why the LLM lost money. All of the LLM's loss sits in its 21 stopped-out trips: -767 USD gross, while the other 44 round trips made +199. But the stops themselves were roughly neutral: holding those positions instead would have changed P&L by -26 / -3 / -59 USD at 4h / 24h / 72h (24h t = -0.02). Every other rule moved P&L by less than 20 USD, within noise. Costs are only 12.6% of the LLM's gross loss (68 of 539 USD). The LLM lost because its entries were wrong, not because the stops, caps or costs hurt it. The daily kill, gross cap and per-name cap never bound, and the sim cannot show bad weekend fills.
